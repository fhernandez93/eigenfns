## 4. The localization section — what to establish and how to word it

Do the analysis, then write only what the data support. Work through these explicitly, each with a verdict "supported / not supported / not testable with this data":

1. **Spatial localization** (ξ ≪ L, small PR): supported for the in-gap and gap-edge states — quote ξ = 1.80–2.51 µm in L = 24.65 µm (ξ/L ≈ 0.08), PR fractions, r² values. State that ξ is an envelope-fit quantity with tens-of-percent fit-range sensitivity, not a transport length.
2. **Anderson vs Lifshitz-tail / rare-region states**: the in-gap states sit in a *pseudogap* of a *single* disorder realization. Distinguish (i) Anderson-localized states of a continuum band from (ii) defect-like states bound to rare local fluctuations of the network (filling-fraction coarse-grained at the ξ scale; the round-4 record **retracted** the rare-region z-test — do not resurrect it as a claim; you may report the coarse-grained ff correlation only as a described diagnostic with its retraction noted). Say plainly which of the two the data can and cannot separate.
3. **Finite-size scaling** — the actual Anderson criterion: PR or ξ vs L at fixed decoration. You have exactly two sizes (N=1000 L=11.44 µm, N=10k L=24.65 µm) with the same circular decoration (`i4int_n1000_*` vs `n10k_G192_*`). Compute the matched-decoration comparison of band-edge ξ (1.5–2.3 vs 1.8–2.1 µm) and PR volume; state whether ξ is L-independent within the fit uncertainty (evidence for true localization rather than a finite-size effect) and that two points do not constitute a scaling analysis. Note 168/210 N=1000 modes are ceiling-limited.
4. **Level statistics** (new, cheap, do it): from the 133 certified N=10k eigenvalues (corrected) and the 611 N=1000 eigenvalues, compute nearest-neighbour spacing distributions and the adjacent-gap ratio ⟨r⟩ (Oganesyan–Huse / Atas et al. 2013) *per spectral band* (below-edge / in-gap / above-edge), compare with Poisson (⟨r⟩ ≈ 0.386) and GOE (≈ 0.536; use GUE 0.603 only if Θ is genuinely complex-Hermitian at Γ with no time-reversal-induced real structure — decide from the operator and justify). Report with bootstrap uncertainties; be explicit that samples of tens of levels give weak statistics and that unfolding across a pseudogap edge is delicate. Also compute the Thouless-style ratio using the finite-difference sensitivity only if it can be obtained from saved data without re-solving (it probably cannot — then say so).
5. **Mobility edge**: not claimable — say why (no transport, no scaling, one realization). Use the existing ξ(ω) funnel as the *phenomenology* of a localization–delocalization crossover across the gap edge, and cite the experimental precedent (Haberko, Froufe-Pérez, Scheffold 2020 — transition from diffusion to localization near the band edge in 3-D amorphous networks) and the theory of gap-edge localization in correlated disorder (Froufe-Pérez et al. PNAS 2017).
6. **Vector-wave caveat**: 3-D Anderson localization of light in uncorrelated point-scatterer media is absent (Skipetrov & Sokolov PRL 2014; Sperling et al. NJP 2016; Skipetrov & Page NJP 2016) and was only recently observed numerically/experimentally in specific systems (Yamilov et al. Nat. Phys. 2023). Position the present result relative to that: localization *near a band gap* of a correlated network is a different mechanism (John 1987; Froufe-Pérez 2017) and is the one this data speaks to.

**Required verdict paragraph** (write your own version from the analysis): the defensible claim is *strongly spatially localized in-gap and gap-edge eigenstates with ξ ≪ L, consistent with Anderson-type localization at the edges of a photonic pseudogap in a correlated disordered network*; the claim of an Anderson transition or a mobility edge requires finite-size scaling over ≥ 3 sizes and/or transport/level-statistics evidence that this data does not contain. If the level statistics computed in step 4 come out cleanly Poisson in-gap and GOE-like well outside, say so with the numbers and upgrade the wording accordingly; if they are inconclusive, say that.
